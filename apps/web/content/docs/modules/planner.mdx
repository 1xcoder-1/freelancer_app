The Planner is where the day gets decided. Open **Planner** in the sidebar
and you get boards — one per focus session — instead of an endless task
list that shames you for yesterday.

## Boards

A board is a short list of what needs doing, made for a day or a work
session. Create as many as you like, rename them, and keep one board per
day or one per project sprint — the planner does not enforce a shape.

Each item on a board is one line: *what needs doing?* That question is the
whole entry form. Everything else is optional planning metadata you attach
to the line:

- **Date** — when this item is meant to happen.
- **Priority** — what to drop first when the day overflows.
- **Project** — link the item to a project so the work shows up where the
  project lives.
- **Timebox** — how long you are giving it. A timebox turns a wish into an
  appointment.

## Day history

Nothing is deleted when the day ends. The planner keeps a **day history**:
past boards stay exactly as they were, and you can look back at any
previous day to see what you actually got done.

- Open a past day from the board's history.
- **Restore this day** brings an old board back as today's starting point
  — useful when a plan was interrupted and deserves a second attempt.

## Sharing a board

Every board can be shared **read-only**. The link shows the board as it
is, with no editing, to anyone who has it — handy for showing a client
what this week looks like without giving them an account or a say.

<Callout type="tip">
Pair the planner with [Time](/docs/modules/time-tracking): a
timebox on a planned item is a natural preview of the timer you are about
to run.
</Callout>
